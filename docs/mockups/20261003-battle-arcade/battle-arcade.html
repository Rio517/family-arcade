Ship Battle: a darker arcade

Start screens, effects and guns, each built from the real game behind an address parameter. Today sits beside every option (dashed frame). Click any picture for full size.

“Revert the Storybook look (tiles, pegs): I don't like the new UI. I liked the little fire we had. I'd prefer it to stay cooler; it's probably people who like battling rather than classic players looking for a board game. Main areas:
  • make that fire nicer;
  • improve the hit so it looks more fun, like an explosion (maybe borrow from the Gulp game);
  • improve the game-start user interface, which kind of sucks. I want a cool, darker ship experience, but blended a little with the playfulness of the arcade: a darker arcade;
  • the water could have been cooler;
  • I'd like to move towards ships rotating their guns and shooting on hits, with a skip option so you don't have to watch the animation every time.”

Try it on the iPad: #/play?look=a, ?look=b, ?look=c for the start screens; #/play?fx=a or ?fx=b for the effects and guns; both together, e.g. #/play?look=a&fx=b. A Pitch switcher then sits in the bottom-left corner to flip between options on the device. look=today&fx=today is the shipped game.

Effects (?fx=)

AArcade: cel-shaded flame tongues, punchy blasts after Gulp's booms with star sparks and “HIT!”, and the Night Ops sea: contour lines, a glowing grid in the water, a sonar ping. BCinematic: a soft roaring blaze, heavier realistic blasts with a flash of light, and the Moonlit Swell: rolling waves, whitecap flecks, the moon's glitter path, stars.

The guns

Our shot: the target locks on the radar, the next ship still afloat trains its guns on the bearing (the enemy's waters lie past our horizon) and fires in a ripple; the shells arc away, then one drops on the radar. Their shot: a warning ring tightens on our water and a shell comes in over the horizon. A tap anywhere skips to the result; Watch the shots (beside the battle log) turns it off for the device, and is off by default under reduced motion. The game never waits on the picture.

The turrets are the ones already in the ship models (named pivots in every GLB); a hinge is added at each gun's breech for elevation and recoil. The Virginia has no gun and fires a missile from a deck hatch; the Ford uses its close-in guns.
